# 🌏 Can Asia Achieve a Common Market? Lessons from the EU and the Role of India

## 📌 Project Overview

This research project investigates whether Asia can progress toward a common market similar to the European Union by evaluating the impact of Free Trade Agreements (FTAs), deep trade integration, and institutional cooperation on bilateral trade flows. Using a panel dataset of 23,770 country-pair observations spanning 2000–2022, the study applies a Poisson Pseudo Maximum Likelihood (PPML) Gravity Model with dyadic and year fixed effects to estimate the causal impact of regional integration on trade performance.

The analysis places particular emphasis on India's role in Asian integration and examines whether deeper trade agreements generate stronger trade outcomes than traditional tariff-based FTAs.

---

## 🎯 Research Objectives

The study aims to answer several key policy questions:

- Can Asia replicate the success of the European Union's common market?
- Do Free Trade Agreements significantly increase trade flows?
- Does deeper institutional integration generate additional trade benefits?
- How does India's participation affect regional trade performance?
- What institutional reforms are required for greater economic integration in Asia?

## 📊 Key Findings

- Free Trade Agreements increase bilateral trade flows by approximately 6–7%.
- Larger economies consistently generate stronger trade relationships.
- Geographic distance remains a significant barrier to trade.
- Deep trade agreements exhibit mixed short-run effects due to institutional adjustment costs.
- India's deep integration agreements underperform relative to expectations.
- Asia lacks the institutional harmonization that supports the EU's common market success.
- Stronger regulatory coordination and governance mechanisms are necessary for deeper regional integration.

## 📈 Methodology

### Gravity Model Specification

The study employs an augmented gravity framework to estimate trade flows:

- Economic Size (GDP)
- Trade Agreements
- Institutional Depth
- Geographic Distance
- Country-Pair Fixed Effects
- Year Fixed Effects

PPML estimation is used to address heteroskedasticity and zero-trade observations, providing more robust estimates than conventional OLS models.

---

## 💼 Policy Implications

- Asian integration requires stronger institutional coordination beyond tariff liberalization.
- Regulatory harmonization is critical for realizing gains from deep trade agreements.
- India can play a leading role in regional integration through improved trade governance.
- Lessons from the European Union suggest that successful common markets require shared institutions, policy convergence, and effective enforcement mechanisms.
